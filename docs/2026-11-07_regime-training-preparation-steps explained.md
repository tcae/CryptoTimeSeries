    The hourly observations are used to classify the overall market’s historical regime—up, flat, down, or unknown. They are not themselves the classifier’s F09 features or trading signals.

    Per coin, at each hourly cutoff
    A cutoff is an observation time, not a threshold: at, say, 12:00, the calculation uses completed minute data through 11:59. With the default settings, it calculates (RegimeCalendar.jl:63):

    Long slope: the normalized price trend over the preceding 10 days.
    Short slope: the normalized price trend over the preceding day; used to detect faster reversals.
    Turnover: average quote turnover over the preceding 10 days; used to weight each coin’s contribution.
    Volatility: variability of minute log returns over the preceding 10 days, scaled to a daily measure; used to assess whether the trend is strong relative to volatility.
    A coin contributes at that hour only if its endpoint is liquid and it has the required contiguous history.

    From coin observations to training sets
    At each hour, the observations from eligible coins are combined into market-wide measures. Turnover determines weights, with each coin capped at 10%; at least 10 coins are required under the default configuration.
    The detector uses the combined slope, volatility and breadth—the weighted share of coins with positive long slope—to identify the market regime. It applies confirmation and hysteresis to avoid switching on every fluctuation. Insufficient coverage produces unknown, not flat (RegimeCalendar.jl:118, RegimeCalendar.jl:180).
    The resulting market timeline is cut into shared calendar blocks. Known-regime blocks are assigned to train, eval or test; unknown periods are excluded. Each coin’s eligible minutes then inherit the assignment of their shared block (RegimeCalendar.jl:222).
    In short: the observations establish market context over time, so training and evaluation can be organized and assessed across market regimes. The hourly observations are temporary preparation data; the resulting shared market timeline and block assignments are persisted as market.arrow and calendar.arrow.
